Health Awareness

Students often choose food based on taste, price, convenience or availability rather than nutritional value. Understanding what your food contains can help you make better choices without giving up what you enjoy.

Understanding nutrition

Carbohydrates are the body's main energy source, found in rice, wheat, poha, bread, potatoes, noodles and pasta. Protein supports growth and repair and comes from pulses, dal, sprouts, beans, milk, curd, peanuts and besan. Healthy fats, needed for various body functions, come from nuts, seeds and certain oils. Vitamins and minerals support immunity, bones and blood formation and are found in fruits, vegetables and pulses. Fibre supports digestion and comes from vegetables, fruits, whole grains and legumes. No single food provides every nutrient the body needs, so eating a variety of foods matters.

Building a balanced diet

A balanced diet means eating different types of food in the right amounts — grains, protein sources, vegetables, fruits, dairy or alternatives, healthy fats, and enough water, every day. Instead of relying only on fried snacks, students can include idli, poha, upma, dosa, sprouts, dal-based foods, vegetables, fruits and besan-based foods regularly. Items like samosa, vada pav, bhaji and burgers can still be enjoyed occasionally, balanced with more nutritious choices — a balanced diet isn't about cutting foods out, it's about choosing well more often.

Choose more often

Lighter on fat, with carbohydrates for energy or protein from dal, sprouts and besan.

Enjoy occasionally

Fried or highly processed items are fine now and then. Balance them with the foods above.

Portion control & limiting processed food

Healthy eating isn't only about what you eat, but how much. Learning to recognise hunger and fullness helps avoid unnecessarily large portions, even of nutritious food. Many popular snacks and drinks are high in added sugar, salt or saturated fat while offering few nutrients — these are best kept occasional rather than a daily default.

Healthy habits worth building

  • Eat meals at reasonably regular times, and avoid skipping meals often
  • Include vegetables and fruits regularly
  • Choose protein-rich foods — pulses, sprouts, beans, milk, curd, nuts
  • Drink enough water through the day
  • Limit sugary drinks, deep-fried snacks and highly processed food
  • Pay attention to portion sizes
  • Check ingredient or nutrition info when it's available
  • Choose freshly prepared, properly stored food when possible

The goal isn't to label food "good" or "bad" — it's to understand that different foods offer different value, and that balance, variety and moderation matter more than any single meal.
